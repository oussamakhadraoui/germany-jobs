## A few honest notes

1. **German is often required for shop-floor jobs.** Installation, field commissioning and production jobs usually need German. R&D, design, testing and research jobs are the most open to English speakers, and his CV fits those best anyway.
2. **Visa:** With a PhD and a job offer, he can apply for the **EU Blue Card**, which has a minimum salary; check the current amount. Without a job offer, the **Chancenkarte (Opportunity Card)** lets him come to Germany to look for work.
3. **Degree recognition:** Look up his university on **anabin** (the German database for recognising foreign degrees) to make sure his degrees are accepted.
4. **Where to search:** LinkedIn with the language filter set to English, arbeitnow.com (English-only jobs), EURAXESS and academicpositions.com (research), and each company's own careers page.
5. **Keep learning German.** Getting to A2 or B1 widens his options a lot.
